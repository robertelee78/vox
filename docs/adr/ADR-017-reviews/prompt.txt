WHAT CHANGED. The withdrawn model: a room's genesis conferred `dial:` on every admitted
member, so joining a room WAS being authorized ("'may this member dial it' and 'is this
person a member' are the same question"). The new model: a service bound to a room is
reachable by exactly the members whose keys its host approved for READING its messages
(ADR-007 per-sender consent), and by nobody else. The `.vox` name changes from the
channelID to the service's own keypair. Discovery becomes a Tor-style client-authorized
descriptor on the room's log, sealed per approved reader.

YOUR JOB IS TO FIND WHAT IS WRONG WITH THE PLAN. Specifically:

1. Is the new authorization model actually sound, or does it move the vulnerability
   rather than remove it? Construct the best attack you can against "reach == the
   host's approved-reader set in this room". Consider: a member approved for reading
   who should not have reach; consent state that is per-author local and may diverge
   between nodes; the interaction with the vouching/`learn_members` path the plan says
   is a separate bug; and whether the host's own view of its consent set can be
   influenced by a peer.

2. Decision 9 claims ONE encryption layer suffices where Tor uses two, because "there
   is no party who holds the descriptor, lacks approval, and knows the name." Is that
   true? Name such a party if one exists. Check the anchor/relay node specifically —
   it is a non-member that serves the board and carries circuits.

3. Decision 9 keys descriptor freshness to the host's ADR-006 `chain_id`. Verify from
   ADR-007 and the code that a consent revocation actually advances it and that the
   channel epoch is genuinely the wrong counter. If the plan is wrong about this, say so.

4. Decision 1 claims a per-service keypair is needed because an approved reader who
   learns name N could otherwise republish N in another room. Is that attack real, and
   does the keypair actually stop it? Is there a cheaper construction that also stops it?

5. The plan says a `.vox` name "is not a bearer capability" and can be pasted into a bug
   report without consequence. Is that true under the plan as written, including for
   someone who is an approved reader in some OTHER room with the same host?

6. Does the plan's own account of what ships today match the code? It claims v0.1.0
   implements the withdrawn model and that the M17.3 gate (`node_m17_up_gate`) asserts
   the vulnerability. Verify both against the tree.

7. What is MISSING from the plan entirely? Look hard at: what happens to a service when
   its host is removed from the room; two hosts binding the same port in one room;
   a host serving into a room it later leaves; and whether `vox forward`/`vox up` still
   have a coherent role.

8. The plan deletes `vox grant` and states that reach cannot be narrowed below "my
   approved readers in this room" nor extended to a non-member. Is deleting it the
   right call, or is there a use that plan does not consider?
