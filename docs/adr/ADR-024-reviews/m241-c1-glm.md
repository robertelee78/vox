## VERDICT

**REVISE**

The three-tier shape, the cap, the trial, and the back-off are sound in principle, and every large-effect measurement is credible in direction. But the queue test demonstrably does not transfer to two of the links in the ADR's own context table, the tier-3 entry condition cannot fire on born-lossy paths, the trial has never been run as a system, and several load-bearing numbers rest on single runs with margins smaller than the rig's documented run-to-run spread. Required changes:

1. Rewrite ADR:105-106 (and the backticked constant names) in "is to" form — `PathSignals`, `BASE_RTT_WINDOW`, `QUEUE_DELAY_MIN`, `QUEUE_DELAY_SHARE`, `GENTLE_LOSS_CAP`, `LOSS_ROUNDS`, `VoxBbr` do not exist in `congestion.rs` (house rule: never state planned work as done).
2. Reconcile ADR:190 with ADR:192 — the 1→2 trigger's "at least 1 s in tier 1" contradicts the dwell rule's "at least 2 s and 20 rounds before any switch".
3. State the comparison flow's datagram size, MTU, and pacing; require the datagram size to match Vox's 8192 (quic.rs:174-176, quic.rs:259) or justify the difference; state the direction and rough size of the stand-in's bias vs kernel TCP (no PRR — cubic.rs:195-196; no HyStart — cubic.rs:111-113; IW 12,000 — cubic.rs:266), since 2× the stand-in is plausibly softer than 2× real TCP on lossy links.
4. Make the queue threshold's blind zone explicit in BDP terms and fix or justify its transfer: at 1 Gbit/s 2 ms and 200 Mbit/s 50 ms the blind zone is 2 BDP (vs 0.4 BDP at the tested link), and the ADR's own 1-BDP arm would be invisible at 50 ms RTT. Either derive the threshold from a delay/BDP budget that transfers, or add arms at those links showing the cap alone suffices. Also address the 10 s windowed minimum adapting to a standing queue > 10 s, which dissolves the delay brake in long-lived congestion.
5. Add a jitter arm (cellular/Wi-Fi-like: typical/min RTT ratio > 1.4, burst jitter above threshold). As specified, such links read as permanently queued: tier 2 unreachable, tier 3 demoted on entry — the target wireless case degrades to tier 1.
6. Add an AQM arm (fq_codel/cake-like early drop with a ~5 ms standing queue): the delay test is structurally blind there and the cap is the only guard; show why the cap suffices when AQM keeps the drop rate under 5% and concentrates drops on the queue-builder.
7. Fix the tier-3 entry's rate condition (ADR:178-180): "below 50% of the best of the last 10 s" is self-referential on a path that was never clean, so tier 3 can never help a born-lossy flow. Tie the condition to path-capacity evidence, not the flow's own past.
8. Address the passed-trial hazard (ADR:181-184): a yielding competitor can let the trial pass on a shallow buffer, after which tier 3 has no exit (rule 3 blind, loss-goes exit unreachable); "at most 2 s per trial" bounds only failed trials. Add a re-check or exit that does not depend on the queue signal, and measure the trial state machine end-to-end before M24.4.
9. Repeat the single-run numbers that sit on bars or threshold margins: 0.93× (the today-red claim), 1.35× and 4.3% (cap), 5.07% vs the 5% cap, 1.01×/1.00× (fairness neutrality), and the noise percentiles/12% — separating emulator timing error (2–6 ms typical, ADR:51-52) from link noise, given the 4 ms threshold.
10. Add bursty random loss to the lossy arms: ~7 consecutive lost 8192-byte datagrams trip the 5%-over-8-rounds cap and force a full cut on random loss; iid 1%/5% understates the cap's bites on real Wi-Fi.
