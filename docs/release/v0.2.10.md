# Release v0.2.10: every known problem fixed

- **Status**: accepted. This is the decider's instruction, 2026-09-26, after v0.2.9 shipped: "we have this weird pattern where we keep finding problems and not solving it and saying oh I guess we'll solve it in the next release but I think now is the time to just solve every known problem in … this current release."
- **Scope**: every known defect or gap in what Vox ships. PRD-001 features that are not built yet are not in scope; they stay in PRD-001's own plan. An unexplained red counts as a known problem.
- **No carry-over**: there is no "carried to v0.2.11" section. If an item cannot be finished, the release waits for it. The only exception is the standing one: something known to be broken may ship only when it is needed to test a feature, and then the release notes say so.
- **Integration owner**: the `vox` session. Each item lands on its own branch, with a real-binary before/after measurement and a mutation check, and an independent verifier accepts it. One integrated release gate runs at the end.
- **Delivery boundary**: an item is delivered when it is included in the published vox v0.2.10 GitHub release.
- **Evidence rule**: a proof must force the condition it measures and print its count even when it passes. A red is classified as one of three things: the product is broken, the proof asserts a withdrawn model, or the proof cannot measure. It is never put down to load.

This is the planning source for the v0.2.10 work items. Each item has a stable ID, and issues quote it. Items that already have a planning source (v0.2.9.md, real-proofs.md, ADR-021, ADR-024) keep their IDs and acceptance there; this document takes them into v0.2.10.

## B. Findings from the v0.2.9 hardening

Each was filed on the findings board (epic #175). The acceptance below replaces the issues' "or bounded by repeated runs" wording: under this release's rule, a known problem gets a cause and a fix, not a rate.

### V210-02 — Sync between two members is not lost while a third holds the room for a member that is down (#159)
**Why.** In `key_package_proof`, sync between C and A was lost in 2 of 3 runs. C held the room for a member that was down while it refused A's pushes 12 times, and A's dropped connection was never redialled.
**Acceptance.** A room's sync is never held by a session with an unreachable member while other members' pushes are refused. A dropped connection to a room member is redialled.
**Validation.** `key_package_proof` passes in repeated runs through the shipped binary. A mutation that restores the hold goes red.

### V210-03 — A correct passphrase is accepted right after a wrong one (#160)
**Why.** Reported once in 43: a correct passphrase was refused straight after a wrong one. The failing output was not saved.
**Acceptance.** A correct passphrase entered right after a wrong one is always accepted. The cause of the reported refusal is identified.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof enters a wrong passphrase and then the right one, in a loop large enough to have caught 1 in 43, and prints its count.

### V210-04 — `udp_tunnel_proof`'s setup reaches its peer, and a relayed restart does not go quiet (#161)
**Why.** 2 of 3 full runs failed in setup (`vox connect` failed after 36–44 s busy). One relayed test timed out with "peer went quiet".
**Acceptance.** `vox connect` in the proof's setup succeeds every time. A relayed restart never ends in `circuit: peer went quiet`. The cause is identified.
**Validation.** `udp_tunnel_proof` passes in repeated runs and prints each run's setup time.

### V210-05 — An unshaped loopback transfer does not collapse (#162)
**Why.** 2 of 45 unshaped loopback transfers ran at 13–14 MB/s on experimental builds.
**Acceptance.** Reproduced on a shipped build and the cause shown, or shown not to occur on the shipped build across a count that would have caught 2 in 45. The measurement is printed either way.
**Validation.** A throughput run of at least 150 unshaped transfers on the release candidate, printing every rate.

### V210-06 — `install_sh_proof` records its failures, and its fast failure is explained (#164)
**Why.** It failed once in 7.3 s, and nothing was captured.
**Acceptance.** The proof records the full output of any failure. The 7.3 s failure's cause is identified.
**Validation.** The proof, run repeatedly in the debug gate, with its capture proved by a forced failure.

### V210-07 — The relay first-connection gate is fast and says which step was slow (#167)
**Why.** A single passing run took 635–1328 s (915 s was not an outlier). One run failed with ProfileBusy. This is also why v0.2.9's release gates overran their time budget.
**Acceptance.** The gate runs within a bound that fits CI's time limit with margin, and reports the slow step when it is slow. The ProfileBusy failure's cause is identified.
**Validation.** Three gate runs with their wall times, plus the step breakdown.

### V210-08 — A post answers promptly while another member is posting (#179)
**Why.** While a peer posts continuously, `vox room post` takes 0.5–5 s at the tail, although the median is ~15 ms. Reads stay at 5–7 ms.
**Acceptance.** `vox room post` answers within a bound that does not depend on peers' sync activity. The bound is the decider's to set; the proposal is p95 under 100 ms and max under 500 ms on loopback.
**Validation.** The `ryw2.sh` and `manyrooms-post.sh` measurements, as a real-binary gate that goes red under a mutation restoring the defect.

### V210-09 — A member who joins through another member is seen promptly by everyone (#180)
**Why.** The third member saw the newcomer 21–28 s late, because a new member travelled only on the 30 s periodic sync.
**Acceptance.** A newcomer is seen by every other online member within a few seconds. Owed work (the anchor publish, the new-member push, a consent retry) is never starved by overlapping sync sessions on a busy room.
**Validation.** `a_new_member_is_seen_promptly_proof` and `a_dead_member_does_not_stall_the_room_proof`, each red under its mutation. A gate keeps a room in continuously overlapping sessions and shows the anchor publish still lands within a bound.

### V210-10 — A join that finds no room on the board names both causes (#181)
**Why.** A join that reached a board holding nothing for the room said the address would not parse.
**Acceptance.** As in #181. Accepted at `9924964`; to be integrated.
**Validation.** `a_room_not_on_the_board_is_named_proof`, both cases, red under both mutations.

### V210-11 — A relayed join finds the room on its anchor's board (#182)
**Why.** In 1 of 12 runs of #40's gate, a relayed join reached its anchor's board and found the room missing.
**Acceptance.** The lost publish is reproduced and its cause fixed. If V210-09's owed-work starvation is the cause, that fix closes it, shown by the same gate.
**Validation.** The relayed-restart gate in repeated runs, printing every join's board fetch result.

### V210-12 — A room of three reads each other without a sender-key wait (#184)
**Why.** A reader waited ~33 s for a member's sender key before anyone had died.
**Acceptance.** Every member of a room of three reads every other member within a stated bound, every run. The cause of the 33 s wait is identified.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof of three members, in repeated runs, printing each first-read time.

## F. The release pipeline

### V210-13 — A release publishes a commit CI already passed
**Why.** Every v0.2.9 release attempt re-ran the full ~60–80 min gates that CI had already run. Two attempts were lost to that alone: one to the job's time limit, one to a proof that CI had not yet run to the end.
**Acceptance.** The release workflow builds, signs and publishes only a commit whose CI run on `main` passed. It does not re-run the full gates.
**Validation.** A tag on a CI-green commit publishes. A tag on a commit without a green CI run is refused, with the reason.

### V210-14 — The gates fit their time limits with margin
**Why.** v0.2.9's gates job hit `timeout-minutes: 60` with nothing red.
**Acceptance.** Every gate job's measured duration is at most two-thirds of its `timeout-minutes`, and the local release check compares them before a tag.
**Validation.** The CI job durations of the release-candidate run, printed against their limits.

### V210-15 — The drain self-filter proof measures, and cannot be fooled by a shared fixture
**Why.** `drain_self_filter_proof` used a fixture shared across trees, which produced false reds for every agent, and it had no CANNOT PROVE classification. The candidate is `d456bf8` (`fix/drain-frames-room-as-data`): per-tree fixture, OpenCode drain framing, and a clean rerun of haiku 20/20 and sonnet 20/20 with 0 misses.
**Acceptance.** The proof runs on its own fixture per tree, classifies an unmeasurable run as CANNOT PROVE, and passes on repeated runs with real models.
**Validation.** The proof's real-model runs, with counts printed, and a mutation that removes the self-filter goes red.

### V210-16 — Every unbounded collection reply is paged
**Why.** #183 paged a room's history. The audit found that the `Rooms` reply reaches the 256 KiB frame at about 1,500 rooms (with 128-byte names), and rooms are unbounded. `Trusted` (~2,600) and `Members` (~7,700) reach it only far beyond today's scale.
**Acceptance.** `Rooms` and `Trusted` replies are paged like `Rows`, so no count of rooms or keyring entries can exceed a frame. `Members` stays bounded by the product's 500-member scale, which is stated where the reply is built.
**Validation.** A real-binary gate with enough rooms to pass one frame lists all of them, and goes red with the paging removed.

### V210-17 — The test hang behind the watchdog is found and fixed
**Why.** ADR-018 §6's watchdog aborts a hung test process, but the hang it was added for (two gate processes at 150% CPU for 21 h) was never diagnosed.
**Acceptance.** The hang is reproduced and its cause fixed, or shown impossible on the current tree. The watchdog stays as a backstop.
**Validation.** The reproduction, before and after.

### V210-18 — A failed attach reports its real error on main
**Why.** causal-order's attach diagnostic `6e38b3c` keeps `IpcClient::open`'s error, but it exists only on `prd1/v030`. Main still loses the cause.
**Acceptance.** On main, a failed attach reports the underlying error.
**Validation.** A real-binary run that makes the attach fail and shows the error.

### V210-19 — No proof is excluded from the blocking gate by name
**Why.** `two_agents_on_separate_processes_join_through_an_anchor_and_talk` (`cross_process_join_proof`) is skipped in CI's blocking `build-test` job as a "known flake" and runs only in the report-only `flaky-watch` job. A known flake is a known problem.
**Acceptance.** The flake's cause is found and fixed, and the proof returns to the blocking gate. The `--skip` and the `flaky-watch` job are removed. A report-only job can no longer fail CI by its time limit.
**Validation.** The proof passes in repeated runs on both OSes, and a mutation that restores the cause goes red.

### V210-20 — Every accepted proof gap is closed
**Why.** CI runs with `VOX_PROOF_ALLOW_UNPROVEN` set to five gaps: `journey.update_replaces_an_older_install`, `install.apple_gate_refuses_unsigned_bytes`, `opencode`, `cross-process-join` and `cross-process-tunnel`. Each is a claim the shipped binary makes that CI does not prove.
**Acceptance.** Each gap is closed by a real proof that runs in CI, and removed from the list. Where a proof needs something CI cannot have (a model login, Apple notarization of deliberately unsigned bytes), the decider records whether it is proved elsewhere, and how.
**Validation.** The list is empty, or it names only what the decider accepted in writing, and every removed name's proof is green in CI.

## G. Defect fixes that exist only on the v0.3.0 branch

prd-tracker's audit of `prd1/v030` (at `6e38b3c`, against v0.2.9 `d9b136c`) found fixes to defects that are present in the shipped v0.2.9. They are ported to main, not left for v0.3.0. (`6e38b3c` is V210-18; `e7d6bae` is covered by V210-15's `7790fd2`.)

### V210-21 — `vox room get` writes only where the receiver says, and never replaces or deletes a file (security)
**Why.** In v0.2.9, `room_cli.rs:1803` takes the destination from the sender's offer name, so another member's offer can name `../../x` or an absolute path. `:1842` truncates the file before verifying it, and `:1869` deletes it on a hash mismatch. There is no read timeout and no size cap. Any member of a room can overwrite, and then delete, a file in the receiver's account. The fix is `7016d3b` on `prd1/v030`.
**Acceptance.** A received file is written only inside a destination the receiver chose, under a name stripped of any path. It never replaces an existing file, is verified before it is placed, and nothing is deleted on a mismatch except the receiver's own temporary file. A read has a timeout and a size cap.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof in which a member offers `../../x`, an absolute path and an existing file's name. Nothing outside the destination is written, nothing existing is changed or deleted, and a mutation that restores the sender-chosen path goes red.

### V210-22 — An agent's drain cannot be forged or flooded
**Why.** In v0.2.9, `agent_hook.rs:205-220` prints a row's text raw after `[hash from author]`, so a post containing a newline and a fake `[… from …]` line appears in an agent's context as another author's message. `:370-376` reads every unread row, and falls back to the whole history on a cursor error, so a busy room floods one prompt. The fix is `2b124f0` on `prd1/v030`.
**Acceptance.** Every line an agent sees is attributed to its true author, and no content can make a line appear to be another author's. A drain is bounded per turn and says how much it held back. A cursor error never re-delivers the whole history.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof that posts a forged-author line and a large backlog, and shows the drain attributes correctly and stays bounded. A mutation that prints raw text goes red.

### V210-23 — Common failures say what went wrong and what to do
**Why.** In v0.2.9, a bind failure is mapped to `MalformedBundle("quic endpoint bind")` (`quic.rs:319`, `:341`, `:442`), so `vox daemon --listen` on a taken port says "could not unlock this profile's identity: Failed(Internal)". `vox forward` onto a taken port waits five minutes. A host's refusal is silent. `trust remove`, and joining a room already held, print enum tokens. The fix is `06ba231` on `prd1/v030`; the join-refusal part is already on main (V29-12).
**Acceptance.** Each of these failures names its cause and the remedy, and fails promptly.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof triggers each failure and asserts the message and the time taken.

### V210-24 — No member can appear as another by matching a short fingerprint prefix (security)
**Why.** Found reviewing V210-22. Authors are shown by a truncated fingerprint: 8 hex characters (32 bits) in the TUI (`live.rs` `short_id`), 8 base32 characters (40 bits) in the agent drain (`agent_hook.rs`), and 12 base32 characters (60 bits) in `coord`. Generating identities until one's prefix matches another member's is feasible at 32–40 bits, and the grinder then appears to be that member.
**Acceptance.** No surface shows an author by a prefix short enough to grind: every surface shows at least 128 bits of the fingerprint (26 base32 characters). The TUI, which holds the keyring, shows the reader's own name for a trusted author, and 26 characters plus "(not in your keyring)" for any other. Surfaces on the control socket (the agent drain, `room read`/`tail`, coord, the board) cannot read the keyring (ADR-020 §3/§7 keeps it behind the identity passphrase), so they make no trust claim either way. Exposing keyring membership over the socket would be a policy change for the decider, and is not part of this item.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: an identity whose short prefix equals a trusted member's (constructed by grinding at the old length, or by substituting the display function's input) posts in a room. The TUI, the agent drain and `vox room read` each show it as not in the keyring and distinct from the trusted member. A mutation back to the short prefix goes red.

### V210-25 — An IPv6-only guest joins without waiting on unusable IPv4 routes (#197)
**Why.** Found by vox-bc. An IPv6-only guest's join waits about 20 s at the board step even when it succeeds, against PRD-001 R42's 2 s. On d9b136c, `reach_a_board` dials routes one at a time (`actor.rs:1187-1199`), starting with the link's IPv4 anchors that this guest cannot use, and each one times out. There may be a second cause: routes deduplicated by anchor identity drop the guest's own IPv6 endpoints when the link and `--anchor` name the same node.
**Acceptance.** An IPv6-only guest reaches a board as fast as a dual-stack one, within R42's 2 s. Unusable routes do not delay usable ones, and no usable endpoint is dropped by deduplication.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof with an IPv6-only guest (the address-family harness) that times the board step, green under 2 s, and red under a mutation restoring sequential dialling.

### V210-26 — Checking the identity passphrase does not stop the node
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021 while building #196. Every `vox trust add`, `list` and `remove` verifies the identity passphrase with production Argon2id inline on the node's actor (`NodeCommand::VerifyPassphrase`). That takes about 0.36 s per call, during which the node answers nothing: no posts, no reads, no syncs. Creating a room already moved its Argon2 seal to a blocking thread; this is the same class of defect.
**Acceptance.** Verifying a passphrase never blocks the actor. Posts, reads and syncs on the same node are served while it runs.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof times `vox room post` and `read` on a daemon while `vox trust list` runs concurrently, and shows them undelayed. A mutation restoring the inline verify goes red.

### V210-27 — The simultaneous-session race is proved, not caught by chance
**Why.** Found verifying F12 (#11). The fix for two members opening pairwise sessions to each other at the same moment (`56f9763`) is guarded only by `room_of_three_keys_proof`, which catches that fix's removal in about 1 run in 3, because nothing forces the two joiners to open at once. A regression would reach CI green about two times in three.
**Acceptance.** A real-binary proof forces both members to open their pairwise sessions on one event, and shows they converge on one session and read each other.
**Validation.** The proof passes repeatedly, and a mutation that disables `56f9763`'s tie-break goes red on every run, not one in three.

### V210-28 — A watchdog abort leaves no process behind
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021. ADR-018 §6's watchdog stops a hung test with SIGABRT, so no `Drop` runs, and every `vox node` and `vox daemon` the test spawned keeps running, reparented to launchd. Aborted runs leaked 3 processes twice in one day, and 2 more orphans were found from another worktree. Leaked daemons load the machine and can hold profiles and ports that later runs then trip over.
**Acceptance.** When the watchdog aborts a test, every process that test spawned is gone as well.
**Validation.** A deliberately hung proof that spawns daemons, aborted by the watchdog, leaves zero `vox` processes. A mutation that restores the plain abort leaves them running.

### V210-29 — A sync failure names its real reason (#202)
**Why.** Found by vox-0e instrumenting #180. `node::channel::sync_failure` wraps every coded sync failure in `Error::MalformedGovernance`, so a peer's ordinary refusal of a colliding session (the commonest failure between two live members) reads "malformed governance struct: sync failed: transport". That points anyone reading it at data corruption, and a collision cannot be told apart from a dead path, so the push retry cannot treat them differently.
**Acceptance.** A sync failure is reported with its own reason, and a refusal because the peer's own session with this node is running carries a distinct reason from a transport failure. No sync failure is reported as a governance or malformed-data error.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof forces a collision and asserts the reason the initiator reports, and a mutation restoring the governance wrapper goes red.

### V210-30 — Trusting a member who is unreachable does not lose them the posts made meanwhile
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021 while forcing #200's race. When a node trusts a member it cannot reach at that moment, the consent is not delivered (`reach_member` returns Unreachable), and `deliver_owed_consents` later builds the sender-key release from the chain's position **at delivery**. Rooms are forward-only, so every post made between the trust and the delivery is unreadable to that member for good; measured, about 25 s of posts. A key that was sent and then refused is re-sent from its original position, so only the never-sent case loses posts. This may also be part of #184.
**Acceptance.** A member trusted while unreachable reads every post made from the moment of trust, once it is reached.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: carol trusts bob while bob is unreachable (the relay between them frozen), carol posts, bob becomes reachable, and bob reads that post. A mutation releasing the key from the delivery-time position goes red.

### V210-33 — A tunnel keeps its packet size through congestion loss (quinn's false black hole)
**Why.** Found by vox on R41 on the CI runners (diagnostic run 36230113835, `VOX_TEST_PATH_STATS`). After congestion losses the QUIC path's MTU drops from 8192 to 1200 on macOS and on Ubuntu. quinn-proto 0.11.14's PLPMTUD `BlackHoleDetector` (upstream main is the same) exempts a loss burst only when its smallest packet is strictly smaller than the acknowledged MTU, and clears suspicion only when a strictly smaller packet is acknowledged. When every packet is already at the maximum size, ordinary congestion bursts count as suspicious, and four of them declare a black hole: 1200-byte packets for `black_hole_cooldown` (60 s). At 1200 bytes, 1 Gbit/s needs about 104k packets a second. The 3-core macOS runner then does WAN at 28–60% of raw, and it explains Ubuntu's earlier 88.7%.
**Constraint (the decider).** quinn is not patched or vendored. The fix is in how Vox configures and calls quinn (for example `MtuDiscoveryConfig`, the probe ceiling or the detector's parameters, or the packet sizes Vox offers).
**Acceptance.** A tunnel under congestion loss keeps sending packets at the path's probed size, and R41 meets its bound on the CI runners where it failed.
**Validation.** R41 through the shipped binary is red on the base on the runner that showed it and green with the fix. A path-stats run shows the MTU never falls below the probed size during a congested transfer. A mutation restoring the default configuration goes red. Owner: vox.
