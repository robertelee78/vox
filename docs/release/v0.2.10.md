# Release v0.2.10: every known problem fixed

- **Status**: accepted. This is the decider's instruction, 2026-09-26, after v0.2.9 shipped: "we have this weird pattern where we keep finding problems and not solving it and saying oh I guess we'll solve it in the next release but I think now is the time to just solve every known problem in … this current release."
- **Scope**: every known defect or gap in what Vox ships. PRD-001 features that are not built yet are not in scope; they stay in PRD-001's own plan. An unexplained red counts as a known problem.
- **No carry-over**: there is no "carried to v0.2.11" section. If an item cannot be finished, the release waits for it. The only exception is the standing one: something known to be broken may ship only when it is needed to test a feature, and then the release notes say so.
- **Integration owner**: the `vox` session. Each item lands on its own branch, with a real-binary before/after measurement and a mutation check, and an independent verifier accepts it. One integrated release gate runs at the end.
- **Delivery boundary**: an item is delivered when it is included in the published vox v0.2.10 GitHub release.
- **Evidence rule**: a proof must force the condition it measures and print its count even when it passes. A red is classified as one of three things: the product is broken, the proof asserts a withdrawn model, or the proof cannot measure. It is never put down to load.

This is the planning source for the v0.2.10 work items. Each item has a stable ID, and issues quote it. Items that already have a planning source (v0.2.9.md, real-proofs.md, ADR-021, ADR-024) keep their IDs and acceptance there; this document takes them into v0.2.10.

## B. Findings from the v0.2.9 hardening

Each was filed on the findings board (epic #175). The acceptance below replaces the issues' "or bounded by repeated runs" wording: under this release's rule, a known problem gets a cause and a fix, not a rate.

### V210-02 — Sync between two members is not lost while a third holds the room for a member that is down (#159)
**Why.** In `key_package_proof`, sync between C and A was lost in 2 of 3 runs. C held the room for a member that was down while it refused A's pushes 12 times, and A's dropped connection was never redialled.
**Acceptance.** A room's sync is never held by a session with an unreachable member while other members' pushes are refused. A dropped connection to a room member is redialled.
**Validation.** `key_package_proof` passes in repeated runs through the shipped binary. A mutation that restores the hold goes red.

### V210-03 — A correct passphrase is accepted right after a wrong one (#160)
**Why.** Reported once in 43: a correct passphrase was refused straight after a wrong one. The failing output was not saved.
**Acceptance.** A correct passphrase entered right after a wrong one is always accepted. The cause of the reported refusal is identified.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof enters a wrong passphrase and then the right one, in a loop large enough to have caught 1 in 43, and prints its count.

### V210-04 — `udp_tunnel_proof`'s setup reaches its peer, and a relayed restart does not go quiet (#161)
**Why.** 2 of 3 full runs failed in setup (`vox connect` failed after 36–44 s busy). One relayed test timed out with "peer went quiet".
**Acceptance.** `vox connect` in the proof's setup succeeds every time. A relayed restart never ends in `circuit: peer went quiet`. The cause is identified.
**Validation.** `udp_tunnel_proof` passes in repeated runs and prints each run's setup time.

### V210-05 — An unshaped loopback transfer does not collapse (#162)
**Why.** 2 of 45 unshaped loopback transfers ran at 13–14 MB/s on experimental builds.
**Acceptance.** Reproduced on a shipped build and the cause shown, or shown not to occur on the shipped build across a count that would have caught 2 in 45. The measurement is printed either way.
**Validation.** A throughput run of at least 150 unshaped transfers on the release candidate, printing every rate.

### V210-06 — `install_sh_proof` records its failures, and its fast failure is explained (#164)
**Why.** It failed once in 7.3 s, and nothing was captured.
**Acceptance.** The proof records the full output of any failure. The 7.3 s failure's cause is identified.

### V210-07 — The relay first-connection gate is fast and says which step was slow (#167)
**Why.** A single passing run took 635–1328 s (915 s was not an outlier). One run failed with ProfileBusy. This is also why v0.2.9's release gates overran their time budget.

### V210-08 — A post answers promptly while another member is posting (#179)
**Why.** While a peer posts continuously, `vox room post` takes 0.5–5 s at the tail, although the median is ~15 ms. Reads stay at 5–7 ms.

### V210-09 — A member who joins through another member is seen promptly by everyone (#180)

**Validation.** `a_new_member_is_seen_promptly_proof` and `a_dead_member_does_not_stall_the_room_proof`, each red under its mutation. A gate keeps a room in continuously overlapping sessions and shows the anchor publish still lands within a bound.

**Acceptance.** As in #181. Accepted at `9924964`; to be integrated.

### V210-12 — A room of three reads each other without a sender-key wait (#184)
**Why.** A reader waited ~33 s for a member's sender key before anyone had died.

## G. Defect fixes that exist only on the v0.3.0 branch

prd-tracker's audit of `prd1/v030` (at `6e38b3c`, against v0.2.9 `d9b136c`) found fixes to defects that are present in the shipped v0.2.9. They are ported to main, not left for v0.3.0. (`6e38b3c` is V210-18; `e7d6bae` is covered by V210-15's `7790fd2`.)

### V210-21 — `vox room get` writes only where the receiver says, and never replaces or deletes a file (security)
**Why.** In v0.2.9, `room_cli.rs:1803` takes the destination from the sender's offer name, so another member's offer can name `../../x` or an absolute path. `:1842` truncates the file before verifying it, and `:1869` deletes it on a hash mismatch. There is no read timeout and no size cap. Any member of a room can overwrite, and then delete, a file in the receiver's account. The fix is `7016d3b` on `prd1/v030`.
**Acceptance.** A received file is written only inside a destination the receiver chose, under a name stripped of any path. It never replaces an existing file, is verified before it is placed, and nothing is deleted on a mismatch except the receiver's own temporary file. A read has a timeout and a size cap.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof in which a member offers `../../x`, an absolute path and an existing file's name. Nothing outside the destination is written, nothing existing is changed or deleted, and a mutation that restores the sender-chosen path goes red.

### V210-22 — An agent's drain cannot be forged or flooded
**Why.** In v0.2.9, `agent_hook.rs:205-220` prints a row's text raw after `[hash from author]`, so a post containing a newline and a fake `[… from …]` line appears in an agent's context as another author's message. `:370-376` reads every unread row, and falls back to the whole history on a cursor error, so a busy room floods one prompt. The fix is `2b124f0` on `prd1/v030`.
**Acceptance.** Every line an agent sees is attributed to its true author, and no content can make a line appear to be another author's. A drain is bounded per turn and says how much it held back. A cursor error never re-delivers the whole history.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof that posts a forged-author line and a large backlog, and shows the drain attributes correctly and stays bounded. A mutation that prints raw text goes red.

### V210-23 — Common failures say what went wrong and what to do
**Why.** In v0.2.9, a bind failure is mapped to `MalformedBundle("quic endpoint bind")` (`quic.rs:319`, `:341`, `:442`), so `vox daemon --listen` on a taken port says "could not unlock this profile's identity: Failed(Internal)". `vox forward` onto a taken port waits five minutes. A host's refusal is silent. `trust remove`, and joining a room already held, print enum tokens. The fix is `06ba231` on `prd1/v030`; the join-refusal part is already on main (V29-12).
**Acceptance.** Each of these failures names its cause and the remedy, and fails promptly.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof triggers each failure and asserts the message and the time taken.

### V210-24 — No member can appear as another by matching a short fingerprint prefix (security)
**Why.** Found reviewing V210-22. Authors are shown by a truncated fingerprint: 8 hex characters (32 bits) in the TUI (`live.rs` `short_id`), 8 base32 characters (40 bits) in the agent drain (`agent_hook.rs`), and 12 base32 characters (60 bits) in `coord`. Generating identities until one's prefix matches another member's is feasible at 32–40 bits, and the grinder then appears to be that member.
**Acceptance.** No surface shows an author by a prefix short enough to grind: every surface shows at least 128 bits of the fingerprint (26 base32 characters). The TUI, which holds the keyring, shows the reader's own name for a trusted author, and 26 characters plus "(not in your keyring)" for any other. Surfaces on the control socket (the agent drain, `room read`/`tail`, coord, the board) cannot read the keyring (ADR-020 §3/§7 keeps it behind the identity passphrase), so they make no trust claim either way. Exposing keyring membership over the socket would be a policy change for the decider, and is not part of this item.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: an identity whose short prefix equals a trusted member's (constructed by grinding at the old length, or by substituting the display function's input) posts in a room. The TUI, the agent drain and `vox room read` each show it as not in the keyring and distinct from the trusted member. A mutation back to the short prefix goes red.

### V210-25 — An IPv6-only guest joins without waiting on unusable IPv4 routes (#197)
**Why.** Found by vox-bc. An IPv6-only guest's join waits about 20 s at the board step even when it succeeds, against PRD-001 R42's 2 s. On d9b136c, `reach_a_board` dials routes one at a time (`actor.rs:1187-1199`), starting with the link's IPv4 anchors that this guest cannot use, and each one times out. There may be a second cause: routes deduplicated by anchor identity drop the guest's own IPv6 endpoints when the link and `--anchor` name the same node.
**Acceptance.** An IPv6-only guest reaches a board as fast as a dual-stack one, within R42's 2 s. Unusable routes do not delay usable ones, and no usable endpoint is dropped by deduplication.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof with an IPv6-only guest (the address-family harness) that times the board step, green under 2 s, and red under a mutation restoring sequential dialling.

### V210-26 — Checking the identity passphrase does not stop the node
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021 while building #196. Every `vox trust add`, `list` and `remove` verifies the identity passphrase with production Argon2id inline on the node's actor (`NodeCommand::VerifyPassphrase`). That takes about 0.36 s per call, during which the node answers nothing: no posts, no reads, no syncs. Creating a room already moved its Argon2 seal to a blocking thread; this is the same class of defect.
**Acceptance.** Verifying a passphrase never blocks the actor. Posts, reads and syncs on the same node are served while it runs.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof times `vox room post` and `read` on a daemon while `vox trust list` runs concurrently, and shows them undelayed. A mutation restoring the inline verify goes red.

### V210-27 — The simultaneous-session race is proved, not caught by chance
**Why.** Found verifying F12 (#11). The fix for two members opening pairwise sessions to each other at the same moment (`56f9763`) is guarded only by `room_of_three_keys_proof`, which catches that fix's removal in about 1 run in 3, because nothing forces the two joiners to open at once. A regression would reach CI green about two times in three.
**Acceptance.** A real-binary proof forces both members to open their pairwise sessions on one event, and shows they converge on one session and read each other.
**Validation.** The proof passes repeatedly, and a mutation that disables `56f9763`'s tie-break goes red on every run, not one in three.

### V210-28 — A watchdog abort leaves no process behind
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021. ADR-018 §6's watchdog stops a hung test with SIGABRT, so no `Drop` runs, and every `vox node` and `vox daemon` the test spawned keeps running, reparented to launchd. Aborted runs leaked 3 processes twice in one day, and 2 more orphans were found from another worktree. Leaked daemons load the machine and can hold profiles and ports that later runs then trip over.
**Acceptance.** When the watchdog aborts a test, every process that test spawned is gone as well.
**Validation.** A deliberately hung proof that spawns daemons, aborted by the watchdog, leaves zero `vox` processes. A mutation that restores the plain abort leaves them running.

### V210-29 — A sync failure names its real reason (#202)
**Why.** Found by vox-0e instrumenting #180. `node::channel::sync_failure` wraps every coded sync failure in `Error::MalformedGovernance`, so a peer's ordinary refusal of a colliding session (the commonest failure between two live members) reads "malformed governance struct: sync failed: transport". That points anyone reading it at data corruption, and a collision cannot be told apart from a dead path, so the push retry cannot treat them differently.
**Acceptance.** A sync failure is reported with its own reason, and a refusal because the peer's own session with this node is running carries a distinct reason from a transport failure. No sync failure is reported as a governance or malformed-data error.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof forces a collision and asserts the reason the initiator reports, and a mutation restoring the governance wrapper goes red.

### V210-30 — Trusting a member who is unreachable does not lose them the posts made meanwhile
**Why.** Found by adr-020_adr-021 while forcing #200's race. When a node trusts a member it cannot reach at that moment, the consent is not delivered (`reach_member` returns Unreachable), and `deliver_owed_consents` later builds the sender-key release from the chain's position **at delivery**. Rooms are forward-only, so every post made between the trust and the delivery is unreadable to that member for good; measured, about 25 s of posts. A key that was sent and then refused is re-sent from its original position, so only the never-sent case loses posts. This may also be part of #184.
**Acceptance.** A member trusted while unreachable reads every post made from the moment of trust, once it is reached.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: carol trusts bob while bob is unreachable (the relay between them frozen), carol posts, bob becomes reachable, and bob reads that post. A mutation releasing the key from the delivery-time position goes red.

### V210-33 — A tunnel keeps its packet size through congestion loss (quinn's false black hole)
**Why.** Found by vox on R41 on the CI runners (diagnostic run 36230113835, `VOX_TEST_PATH_STATS`). After congestion losses the QUIC path's MTU drops from 8192 to 1200 on macOS and on Ubuntu. quinn-proto 0.11.14's PLPMTUD `BlackHoleDetector` (upstream main is the same) exempts a loss burst only when its smallest packet is strictly smaller than the acknowledged MTU, and clears suspicion only when a strictly smaller packet is acknowledged. When every packet is already at the maximum size, ordinary congestion bursts count as suspicious, and four of them declare a black hole: 1200-byte packets for `black_hole_cooldown` (60 s). At 1200 bytes, 1 Gbit/s needs about 104k packets a second. The 3-core macOS runner then does WAN at 28–60% of raw, and it explains Ubuntu's earlier 88.7%.
**Constraint (the decider).** quinn is not patched or vendored. The fix is in how Vox configures and calls quinn (for example `MtuDiscoveryConfig`, the probe ceiling or the detector's parameters, or the packet sizes Vox offers).
**Acceptance.** A tunnel under congestion loss keeps sending packets at the path's probed size, and R41 meets its bound on the CI runners where it failed.
**Validation.** R41 through the shipped binary is red on the base on the runner that showed it and green with the fix. A path-stats run shows the MTU never falls below the probed size during a congested transfer. A mutation restoring the default configuration goes red. Owner: vox.

## I. Found during v0.2.10

### V210-34 — Sync is scheduled like a switch, not a hub (ADR-025)
**Why.** The decider asked for sync to behave like a switch ("slots and queues"), not a hub. Today two members posting at once refuse each other's sessions and retry at random, and a push past the 16-slot cap is skipped, not queued. Four real defects sit in the same machinery:
- the slot-cap skip;
- a truncated serve counted as done;
- a failed partial apply that doesn't wake the room's other peers;
- a receiver that stores entries it did not ask for.

### V210-36 — A hung proof's dump shows the process that is hung (watchdog follow-up to #190)
**Why.** Found by vox-0e on #190. When a proof passes its budget, the test watchdog samples every thread of the **test process** and then kills its descendants (`support/watchdog.rs`, `fire`). ### V210-37 — A key that arrives before its consent does not leave earlier posts unrendered (#210)

### V210-40 — Every at-rest secret is sealed as strongly as the identity (post-quantum)
**Why.** Found by vox-0e while building V210-35, and widened by adr-020_adr-021 before building. ### V210-63 — Equivocation below the head is detected, not left as a silent stall
**Why.** Found by vox-0e, checking whether ADR-025 regressed fork detection (it did not). Equivocation at the head (equal lengths, different head hashes) is detected by `wants_for`, which asks for the peer's head, and `dag.rs` then freezes the equivocator. Divergence below the head, with unequal lengths, is never detected. Example: bob holds X at p and has gone on to p+2, while carol holds X′ at p. Carol asks for p+1..p+2, and p+1 is refused as `Rejected::Feed` (prev mismatch), which is not a fork proof. Bob asks carol for nothing. Carol stays stuck behind, and no equivocation is raised. The same gap is in 1de7548 and in v0.2.9, so it is shipped.
**Acceptance.** When a member's feed diverges from a peer's below the head (a prev-link mismatch on an asked range), the node fetches the peer's entry at the divergence point. If two validly signed entries share a position, it raises the same equivocation outcome as at the head, freezing the attributable equivocator. An honest member is never stuck silently behind a fork.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof with a staged below-head equivocation (a raw client signing a second entry at a position one member has built past), in which the fork is detected and the equivocator is frozen on both members. A mutation that restores the silent `Rejected::Feed` goes red.

### V210-64 — A real clock step behind a predecessor is cured on both clocks, with drift bounded
**Why.** Found by the independent verifier of V210-61 (#250). The fix `13500c3` moves only the millisecond clock, which floors `seq`. The record `timestamp` comes from the seconds clock, which a real clock step also moves, so a lag of more than about 3 s is still refused on the timestamp. Separately, `seq` can drift ahead without limit when two live processes of one identity clash.
**Acceptance.** A fresh process whose clock steps behind its predecessor's is taken by its board after the bounded republishes: both the `seq` floor and the timestamp floor move forward, each clamped to at most about 16 s ahead of the clock. Two live processes of one identity cannot drive either floor ahead without bound.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof in which the test skew moves both clocks, as a real step does, with a lag beyond 3 s. It is cured within the republish budget. A clash between two live processes stays within the clamp. Mutations that move only `seq`, or that remove the clamp, go red.

### V210-65 — The join proof catches a slow host, wherever the host's delay lands
**Why.** Found by the independent verifier of V210-62 (#251) and confirmed by vox-0e. A mutant that delays every inbound handshake by 6 s passes at the 12 s bound, and passed 1 of 2 runs even at 5 s: the delay lands in join steps other than dial and exchange, which are all the proof bounds.
**Acceptance.** The proof bounds every part of the join except the joiner's own solve and seal, at about 5 s, so a host that delays any step of the join is caught.
**Validation.** The inbound-handshake-delay mutant goes red. The D8 mutant stays red. The candidate is green over repeated runs.

### V210-66 — Equivocation: the anchor keeps it, the TUI names every one, and the proof's restart cannot be fooled
**Why.** Found by the independent verifier of V210-63 (#252), and not blocking that item, but known problems:
- (a) The proof's restart step does not rule out carol meeting the other side of the fork again. A live peer that holds the other side could re-detect the fork and turn the no-persistence mutant green.
- (b) An anchor keeps no fork proofs, so a restarted anchor forgets the freeze. Members still refuse the author.
- (c) The TUI names only the first equivocation in a room.
**Acceptance.**
- A restarted node lists a fork with no peer able to show it again.
- An anchor keeps and restores its fork proofs as a member does, as the small signed pair it already verified, with no new class of room data.
- The TUI names every equivocation in the room.
**Validation.**
- The proof stops every other node before carol restarts, and asserts that the fork is still listed. The no-persistence mutant goes red.
- An anchor restart keeps the freeze. A mutant without anchor persistence goes red.
- A room with two equivocators shows both notices in the TUI.

### V210-67 — The work-version proof tests the version, not a race
**Why.** Found by adr-020 from CI run 36545340832 (macOS, `869d8f0`). `work_version_proof`'s "another version" arm hard-codes `0.2.9`. On a build that is itself 0.2.9, that stamp matches, coordination is correctly allowed, and the proof waits out its deadline for a refusal that cannot come. Its green runs passed by a race: the predicate `stderr.contains("0.2.9")` also matched a stale refusal for the earlier "banana" arm, since every refusal says "required 0.2.9". So the version check was never tested. The product is correct.
**Acceptance.** The proof's other version is derived from the running build and never equals it. The refusal it accepts must name that worker's stamp (`runs vox <stamp>`), which no other refusal text can satisfy.
**Validation.** The new proof is green on 0.2.10 and on a tree forced to 0.2.9. A product mutant that treats another valid version as a match goes red.

### V210-68 — A node renews its own address record before it expires
**Why.** Found by the independent verifier of #179's attempt 2 (`179eefe`). Nothing renews a node's own address record (2 h lifetime) on its anchors before it expires. Before `179eefe`, a busy room renewed it by accident on every sync that applied entries. That publish round was a storm, and #179 removed it. Now a room with only content traffic never renews it, and an idle room never did. After 2 h the anchor drops the record, and peers can no longer find the node through the board.
**Acceptance.** A node republishes its own address and bundle records to every anchor well before they expire (for example at half their lifetime), whatever the room's traffic. The republish is bounded: one round per anchor per renewal interval, never a storm.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof that shortens the lifetime with a test-only knob, as the other test knobs do (inert when unset). An idle node's record is still held by the anchor past several lifetimes, and a peer can still reach it through the board. The renewal count stays within its bound. A mutation that removes the renewal goes red.

### V210-69 — An anchor stops on Ctrl-C even when the signal lands with its tick
**Why.** Found by vox-0e while investigating #257 (v0.3.0 CI). `vox node`, `forward`, `serve`, `up` and the room offer create a new `ctrl_c()` listener on every select-loop turn. A SIGINT that arrives in the same turn as the 500 ms tick is dropped, and the process keeps serving. Reproduced with the shipped binary: 13 of 20 anchors never exited.
**Acceptance.** Every long-running verb installs its Ctrl-C listener once, and exits on the first SIGINT whenever it arrives.
**Validation.** `an_anchor_stops_on_ctrl_c_proof` through the shipped binary, with many SIGINTs timed against the tick. Every process exits. A mutation that restores the per-turn listener goes red.

## The v0.2.10 defect sweep (2026-09-29)

At the decider's request, all agents reviewed the whole of `integrate/v0.2.10` @ `2ba591f` read-only, section by section, for defects of the ten classes the last week's reds had shown. Each confirmed finding is grouped into one item below by the fix that closes it. Unconfirmed suspicions are listed under "to check" in the item they belong to.

### V210-70 — A board admits as Member only verified room members, and a stranger cannot abuse it
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (node networking and vox-0e's nat/transport review).
- **Self-minted geneses make a stranger a Member.** An unknown peer publishes a genesis it minted itself, and `classify` (network.rs:558-604) then treats it as a `Member`: an open relay between the attacker's own identities, circuit slots exhausted for real members (`relays_for`), member endpoints disclosed and dials steered through RELAY coord, and fake rooms in `vox status`. It also defeats 8097148.
- **The genesis board fills and never drains** (store.rs:371, 4096): later rooms cannot be joined through that node, and the refusal is swallowed (actor.rs:3505).
- **Pre-join slots can be filled by anyone** who knows the room id (store.rs:434, 256 slots, no PoW), refusing real joiners for 2 h.
- **A member can vouch any key onto another node's board** without a witness check, and that key then classes as Member (service.rs:529); the mirror loop does this unintentionally.
- **`RendezvousStore::prune_expired` is never called** (store.rs:523).
- **To check:** membership on the board reads only epoch 0 after an M18.1 rotation.
**Acceptance.** A peer is classed Member on a board only through admission evidence the board verifies (a witnessed or root-signed admission), never through a genesis it authored or a record another member vouched without a witness. The genesis and pre-join stores are bounded without letting strangers push out the node's own rooms or real joiners. Expired records are pruned. No stranger can use a node as a relay or list fake rooms in its status.
**Validation.** Real-binary proofs: a stranger that publishes its own genesis cannot open a circuit or appear in status; a flood of stranger geneses or pre-joins does not stop a real room's join through that node; a vouched-without-witness key is not a Member. Each with a mutation that restores the hole going red.

### V210-71 — No peer, and no slow operation, can stall the node's actor or a room lock
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep.
- **Pairwise frames are read on the actor.** `take_inbound_skdm` → `recv_pairwise`, with 30 s + 30 s patience (actor.rs:4219, 7188, 7274). A silent peer freezes the node for up to 120 s per stream. Two reviewers found this independently.
- **SKDM and KEY_TAKEN writes are awaited on the actor** with no write timeout (actor.rs:7224).
- **`vox room open` and `vox serve` run Argon2id on the actor** (actor.rs:7684, 7528).
- **`admit_board_records` re-verifies every board record** under the room lock on every outbound session (actor.rs:8447).
- **The governance evaluator is rebuilt from scratch per synced governance entry.** Each rebuild does an O(N³) closure plus re-verifying every signature under the room lock (evaluator.rs:904-942, 240; channel.rs:2779, 2968, 3446), so a joiner's first sync in a 20+ member room takes minutes.
- **`view()` clones every room's whole timeline per IPC read page** under the watch lock (ipc.rs:1652, actor.rs:1770); `room tail` re-reads everything on each event.
- **A drain that exceeds its budget** reports "sync mode unsupported" and drops the staged entries (sync.rs:1226).
**Acceptance.** No inbound stream's reads, and no network write, happen on the actor. Argon2id, bulk signature verification and evaluator rebuilds run off the actor and outside room locks, or incrementally. An IPC read never clones every timeline. A post's latency is unaffected by any of these. A drain over its budget applies what it staged and names the timeout.
**Validation.** Real-binary proofs: a peer that opens a pairwise stream and stays silent does not delay another member's post beyond V210-08's bound; a governance-heavy room (tens of consents) is joined within a bound; a post stays within V210-08's bound while a room is opened with its passphrase. Each with a mutation that restores the on-actor or under-lock work going red.

### V210-72 — The control socket, passphrases and shared files cannot leak to another local user or process
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (the CLI review and adr-020's at-rest review, independently).
- **`vox room send` leaves its file service registered** on any exit except Ctrl-C (room_cli.rs:1718-1800, persisted). A later process on that port is exposed to members, and MAX_SERVICES fills up.
- **On Linux a long path makes the control socket fall back to `/tmp`** under a predictable name, and clients never check the owner or peer credentials (paths.rs:126-141, ipc.rs:1403, room_cli.rs:43). Passphrases can go to another user, or the daemon cannot start.
- **The socket is bound with the default mode, then chmod'ed 0600**, which leaves a window (ipc.rs:1410).
- **`vox shell-setup` rewrites the rc file 0644** and replaces a symlink with a plain file (shell.rs:172-258).
- **One `accept()` error ends the control socket for good** while the daemon runs, and the error text afterwards is false (ipc.rs:1427).
- **The room passphrase is accepted via `--passphrase` and `VOX_ROOM_PASSPHRASE`** (cli.rs:809, 989), unlike the identity passphrase.
- **An interrupted `vox room get` leaves the daemon's forward open** (room_cli.rs:1906).
**Acceptance.** A file offer and a get are withdrawn on every way the verb ends. The control socket lives only in a directory private to the user, is created 0600 from the start, and a client refuses a socket not owned by the user. The accept loop survives transient errors. shell-setup keeps the rc file's mode and symlink. Room passphrases are never taken from argv or the environment.
**Validation.** Real-binary proofs per point: SIGTERM or SIGHUP of `room send` and `room get` leaves no service or forward; a socket path over the limit is private and owned; an accept error (fd exhaustion) does not end the socket; shell-setup keeps 0600 and a symlink. Mutations restoring each go red.

### V210-73 — A received message is never overwritten after a restart
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (channel/log review).
On open, `next_log_id` is taken from the LogDb ids only (channel.rs:1231), while a received message's rendered-text cache row takes the next id after its log row (channel.rs:3351). After a restart, the next `append_text` writes its log and cache rows under that id (channel.rs:3521), and redb's insert silently overwrites the earlier message's cache row. The message then disappears from `vox room read` after the next restart, for good. It is silent data loss in ordinary use.
**Acceptance.** Row ids for every segment kind of a room are allocated from one counter that survives reopen, so no row ever overwrites another. The fix also repairs or detects collisions already on disk.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: bob receives alice's message; bob restarts, posts, and restarts again; alice's message is still read. A mutation restoring the LogDb-only id goes red.

### V210-74 — A hostile entry cannot freeze, brick or silently empty a room
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (channel/log review).
- **A validly signed but unclassifiable entry** (wrong channel or epoch in a governance body) is logged, then refused. The room's sync is treated as poisoned until a reopen that never comes, later entries in the pass are left as a feed gap, and after a restart `open` fails, so the room never opens (channel.rs:2943-2995, 1220; actor.rs:6414).
- **A peer serving sync can strip an entry's payload.** The signature covers only the skeleton, so the stripped entry is accepted and fills its position; it is never logged and never refetched, and it spreads onward. The author's later entries then leave a gap, and `open` fails (entry.rs:612, sync.rs:1366, channel.rs:2885).
- **To check:** `gov_entries.push` before a failing rebuild; a render that advances the chain without persisting it.
**Acceptance.** An entry whose payload is absent, or does not bind to the signed skeleton, is never accepted as held. An entry that verifies but cannot be classified is refused before anything is persisted, and neither poisons the room nor leaves a gap. A room with such an entry on disk still opens, reporting the entry. One member's bad entry never stops other members' sync.
**Validation.** Real-binary proofs with a hostile client: a payload-stripped entry is refused and the real one still arrives; an unclassifiable signed entry leaves the room syncing and openable after a restart. Mutations restoring each go red.

### V210-75 — Anchors and port mappings are renewed and followed for the life of the node
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (actor and nat reviews).
- **A room's learned anchor is never redialled after it drops.** It is not in the configured set (actor.rs:3315-3458). `reach_for_sync` gets no board endpoints for an anchor, and nothing republishes on that reconnect.
- **One failed port-mapping renewal ends renewal for good** (`renew_at(now, &[])` gives None) (actor.rs:3834, 345).
- **An anchor's address list only grows.** Past 8 it cannot follow a moved anchor, TooLong comes back every 30 s, and the stale list is persisted (bootstrap.rs:167).
- **One unresolvable hostname in the anchors file fails every anchor**, with a wrong error and no line number, and the refresh loop skips all updates (link.rs:272).
- **To check:** a blocking DNS lookup in the async refresh.
**Acceptance.** Every anchor a node uses, configured or learned from a room, is redialled with backoff after any loss, and the node republishes its records on reconnect. A failed mapping renewal is retried with backoff. An anchor's addresses are replaced with the current ones, not accumulated. A bad anchors-file line is skipped and named with its line number, and does not stop the others.
**Validation.** Real-binary proofs: a member whose only anchor came from the invite reaches it again after the anchor restarts; a moved anchor with a new address is followed. Mutations restoring each go red. Mapping renewal by code review plus a proof if the harness allows.

### V210-76 — Locking wipes every secret, and consent is recorded atomically
**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (two actor reviewers, and the channel review).
- **The outbound `Joiner` is not tracked or aborted at lock.** It is an untracked `tokio::spawn` (actor.rs:4796), so it keeps the vault signer, the prekey ring and the room passphrase for tens of seconds after lock. `begin_create_channel` keeps the signer too.
- **Held consent sender keys (`consent_keys`) survive lock** and are not Zeroizing (actor.rs:7349, pending_consent.rs).
- **Consent writes the grant, `delivered` and `entitled` in three separate commits** (channel.rs:1985-2001). A crash or failed write before `SEG_ENTITLED` makes a restart release the live generation from its origin, which is pre-trust history (channel.rs:2549).
**Acceptance.** Lock aborts every task that holds identity or room secrets, and zeroizes held consent keys. A consent's grant, delivered and entitled records are committed in one transaction, and no failure between them can widen what a member reads.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: lock during a slow join, then show the join does not complete and nothing is signed after lock. Code review for zeroization. A consent interrupted by a kill at every point never releases pre-trust posts (#214's kill-point method). Mutations go red.

### V210-77 — Prekeys, pending consents and a new profile stay usable for the life of the node
**Why.** Found by adr-020's at-rest review in the v0.2.10 sweep.
- **`PrekeyRing::maintain` runs only at unlock** (prekeys.rs:351, 616). A daemon up for weeks never prunes consumed one-time prekeys, runs out after 64, never rotates the signed prekey, and rewrites a growing ring on every consume.

- **To check:** creating an identity over a leftover store.
**Acceptance.** A running node maintains its prekey ring periodically: it prunes, refills and rotates. Pending consents are bounded consistently on save and load, and never block unlock. A half-created profile is repaired or re-creatable. Memory locking is reference-counted per page or otherwise correct.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof with a test-only clock knob: the ring rotates and refills while the daemon runs past the rotation period, and sessions still get one-time prekeys after more than 64. Code review and a staged-failure proof for profile create. Mutations go red.

**Why.** Found by the v0.2.10 defect sweep (actor review).

### V210-85 — Every `vox connect` failure says why

### V210-86 — After an anchor restarts, every member is back promptly
**Why.** Found by fix-v210-70 while flooding a shipped `vox node` (2026-09-29). With 300 distinct identities connecting at once, only 107–188 connected per run; every failure was `CONNECTION_REFUSED`. The accept loop deliberately refuses validated handshakes past `HANDSHAKES_IN_FLIGHT = 64` (node/actor.rs:1160) instead of queueing them, and the dialler turns any failed handshake into `SignatureInvalid` (transport/quic.rs:509). **Acceptance.** A refused dial reads as a refusal, never a bad signature (covered by #272 if its `Error::Handshake` reaches this path). A refused peer retries promptly (not backed off like an auth failure), or the node answers "not now" in a way that brings it back quickly. After an anchor restart with 300 members redialling at once, every member is connected again within a hard-coded bound, and the cap on concurrent handshakes still holds.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof: 300 identities against a shipped `vox node`, restarted, with the count reconnected and the time to the last one asserted; a timing proof, so under the lock. ### V210-94 — A lock wipes every secret at once, including those held by work still in flight
**Why.** Found by verifier-267 while verifying #267 (V210-76, accepted at f2070aa, https://github.com/robertelee78/vox/issues/267#issuecomment-5914353847).

### V210-96 — An address is printed only once its room can be joined through it

### V210-107 — Nothing requires an anchor when the peer can be reached directly
**Why.** Filed as #302. The design since 2026-06-19 (ADR-012), restated by the decider on 2026-10-01: "anchors effectively bridge hosts that can't otherwise find each other"; "for all other use cases, direct is fine, sans anchor". The anchor sweep at integrate 69616f40 found the code has drifted from this. This item covers the drift outside V210-96's serve, invite and join path:

### V210-108 — Every long-running vox verb stops cleanly on SIGINT, SIGTERM, SIGHUP and SIGQUIT
**Why.** Filed as #303. The decider's ruling (2026-09-30): SIGHUP always means a clean stop, because users launch from tmux or systemd. verifier-277c2 found, while verifying #277 (https://github.com/robertelee78/vox/issues/277#issuecomment-5930676860), that `vox serve` dies silently on SIGHUP and SIGTERM, `vox daemon` ignores SIGHUP, and `vox up` and `vox forward` handle only Ctrl-C. #277 c2's `StopSignal`/`stop_requested` (app.rs, no `unsafe`) is the mechanism to reuse.
**Acceptance.** Every long-running vox verb treats SIGINT, SIGTERM, SIGHUP and SIGQUIT as a clean stop: it says it was stopped, exits with the signal's code, and leaves no processes behind.
**Validation.** Extend the existing stop proofs (for example `an_anchor_stops_on_ctrl_c_proof` and `daemon_shutdown_proof`) rather than adding files. Send each signal to each verb and assert what the person sees: the stop message, the exit code, and 0 processes left. Every red names product or test. Run once; one mutant per claim (for example, SIGHUP not taken) goes red.

### V210-109 — A tunnel keeps its speed on a Mac that is briefly paused
**Why.** Found by ac-fix98 while working on V210-98 (#294), on macOS CI. Pausing both vox processes for 5 ms in every 20 ms drops the tunnel's LAN throughput on macOS to a mean of 75% of raw (0 of 12 runs at 90% or more). Under the same stress, Linux holds 94–99.8%. A busy Mac pauses programs like this in ordinary use, and throughput is a headline product value. The best change found so far (no restart-after-idle) lifts macOS to about 86%; a 32 MiB stream window, turning off the fast Apple datapath, and 1452-byte datagrams made no stable difference.
**Acceptance.** Under the same stress, the tunnel keeps at least 90% of raw on macOS, without a loss on Linux or on the WAN arm. Cubic stays, security is never traded away, and no dependency is patched.
**Validation.** The optional R41 proof (cargo feature `optional-proofs`), run on macOS with the pause stress, before and after, side by side with the baseline. Every red names the emulator or vox (V210-98's attribution). Optional proofs block nowhere, so this item is closed on that evidence plus review.
