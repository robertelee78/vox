# Release hardening: v0.2.8 and v0.2.9

- **Status**: accepted — the decider's standing instruction, 2026-09-24: "fix all known issues and cut a new release", and "shipping things that we know are broken should only be done if we need it for testing of a feature".
- **Integration owner**: the `vox` session. Each item lands on its own branch with a real-binary before/after measurement; one integrated release gate runs at the end.
- **Delivery boundary**: an item is delivered when it is included in a published vox GitHub release.

This is the planning source for the release-hardening work items. Each item has a stable ID; issues quote it.

## Shipped in v0.2.8

v0.2.7 was tagged and never published: its release gate caught V28-01. Everything on `main` since v0.2.6, plus the items below, ships as v0.2.8.

### V28-01 — A room's direct sync is owed, not skipped, while its anchor sync runs
**Why.** A room's in-flight sync mark let a failing anchor session take the room every round, so the direct member-to-member sync was skipped every round and two members stopped exchanging messages for good.
**Acceptance.** In the configuration that starved the room (both members visiting the anchor first), messages cross; a skipped peer is retried a tick later.
**Validation.** `node_m15_anchor_gate` with the anchor forced to sort first: fails before, passes after, delivery about 2s.

### V28-02 — A second joiner is not locked out, and both ends of a duplicate connection agree
**Why.** The accept loop handled one handshake at a time, so a second person joining right after the first was locked out for 30s; and a node's duplicate connections were resolved by arrival order, so the two ends could keep different ones.
**Acceptance.** A host with two back-to-back joiners admits the second; duplicate-connection decisions agree at both ends.
**Validation.** `order4.sh` second joiner in 5/5 (was 0/5); both-ends duplicate logging 0 of 28 pairs disagreeing (was 2 of 5); `service_rehearsal_proof` back in the blocking gate.

### V28-03 — A board does not refuse a record it already holds
**Why.** Re-announcing an unchanged record was reported as a refusal, and a changed record (a routable address, a new prekey bundle) was refused for up to 60s.
**Acceptance.** An unchanged record is a no-op; a changed record is accepted immediately; stale mirrors are not reported as refusals.
**Validation.** Real-binary two-member run: refresh-floor refusals 193 to 0.

### V28-04 — Opening a stream cannot wait forever
**Why.** `open_bi` had no deadline, and a dead connection was reported as an internal error.
**Acceptance.** Opening a stream is bounded; a closed connection reports `Unreachable`.
**Validation.** Release gate plus real-binary runs.

### V28-05 — A daemon started as another vox releases the profile waits for it
**Why.** `vox daemon` opened the profile once and exited on "another vox already has this profile open" even when the other vox was finishing.
**Acceptance.** The daemon retries a busy profile briefly; a profile that stays held still gets the same message.
**Validation.** `remote_interrupt_proof` 5/5 on the release tree (was 3/3 failing).

### V28-06 — A peer's streams are not served one at a time
**Why.** The per-connection stream loop served rendezvous, coordination and circuit streams inline, so a member's next board put waited behind its last stream and nodes froze for 30–80s; a relay's stream to its target waited the same way.
**Acceptance.** Rendezvous, coordination and circuit streams are served concurrently; ordering-sensitive streams keep arrival order.
**Validation.** Paired real-binary runs: freezes 60s/60s/21s to one 20s; `service_rehearsal_proof` 4/5 to 5/5.

## Planned for v0.2.9

### V29-01 — A member's node does not freeze while passing records on
**Why.** A sync session holds its room's lock for its whole run, so an anchor publish waiting for that lock stalls the actor for up to the 20s frame timeout.
**Acceptance.** No actor stall attributable to publishing; the exchange does not hold the room lock across network waits.
**Validation.** Real-binary `peer.sh`: zero `busy … passing on a record` stalls.

### V29-02 — The posting quota is removed
**Why.** PRD-001 R3 removes per-author rate limits.
**Acceptance.** No per-author rate refusal remains.
**Validation.** Release gate.

### V29-03 — A non-member is not served a room's sync
**Why.** Inbound sync was served to a peer that is not a member of the room.
**Acceptance.** An inbound sync for a room the peer is not in is refused.
**Validation.** A gate with a non-member requesting a room's sync.

### V29-04 — A fresh connection is not pushed rooms the peer is not in
**Why.** `sync_one` pushed every room to a fresh connection, including rooms the peer is not a member of.
**Acceptance.** Only rooms shared with the peer are pushed.
**Validation.** Real-binary count of sessions and entries pushed to a non-member: zero.

### V29-05 — A restarted peer's dead connection is replaced without reopening the duplicate mismatch
**Why.** An anchor could keep a restarted host's dead connection; the proposed address-based restart rule disagrees between the two ends under NAT rebinding.
**Acceptance.** A restarted peer is reachable promptly, and a live duplicate is always resolved identically at both ends.
**Validation.** A virtual-network mode that rebinds a NAT port mid-run, plus a restart gate.

### V29-06 — A chat message is pushed on append, not on the next tick
**Why.** A posted message waited for the 1s tick before it was pushed.
**Acceptance.** A local append triggers an immediate push.
**Validation.** `perf_r40_*`: median push latency near zero.

### V29-07 — A message over a relay is delivered
**Why.** A message over a relay was not delivered in 3 of 7 runs.
**Acceptance.** Messages over a relay are delivered.
**Validation.** A relayed real-binary run, repeated.

### V29-08 — Joining a room does not stall the actor
**Why.** The actor stalled about 10–16s while joining a room.
**Acceptance.** No actor stall attributable to a join.
**Validation.** Paired runs recording `Stalled` events.

### V29-09 — Creating a room does not stall the actor
**Why.** The actor stalled about 5s while creating a room.
**Acceptance.** No actor stall attributable to creating a room.
**Validation.** Paired runs recording `Stalled` events.

### V29-10 — A long room stays readable to a newcomer
**Why.** A room with more than 1000 messages from one author is suspected unreadable to newcomers.
**Acceptance.** A newcomer reads a room past 1000 messages from one author.
**Validation.** A product proof with a room past that size.

### V29-11 — A second joiner stays admitted, as a permanent regression gate
**Why.** The accept-loop fix was proven with a script; the back-to-back join gate makes it permanent.
**Acceptance.** The back-to-back join gate runs in the blocking release gate.
**Validation.** The gate fails with an inline accept loop and passes with the concurrent one.

### V29-12 — A wrong-passphrase join is reported as refused
**Why.** `node_m14_gate:178` saw `Failed(Internal)` for a wrong-passphrase join about 1 in 20.
**Acceptance.** A wrong-passphrase join is reported `Refused` in repeated runs.
**Validation.** `node_m14_gate` repeated under load.

### V29-13 — A log entry is proven to cross between processes through an anchor
**Why.** `cross_process_join_proof` has been excluded from the release gate.
**Acceptance.** The proof passes and returns to the blocking gate.
**Validation.** Repeated runs, paired against v0.2.8.

### V29-14 — A relayed pair is proven to find a direct path
**Why.** `relayed_path_is_retried` has been excluded from the release gate.
**Acceptance.** The proof passes and returns to the blocking gate.
**Validation.** Repeated runs, paired against v0.2.8.

### V29-15 — Both ends agree on a duplicate even when they classify its path differently
**Why.** The tie-break's better-path branch compares each end's own path classification, so two ends that classify one connection differently could disagree.
**Acceptance.** A duplicate is resolved identically at both ends regardless of path classification.
**Validation.** Both-ends duplicate logging.

### V29-16 — Shutting a node down releases its profile before it reports done
**Why.** `Shutdown` replies before cleanup, and something kept the store file open for milliseconds after the store was dropped.
**Acceptance.** When `Shutdown` reports done, the profile can be opened by another process.
**Validation.** Shut down and immediately reopen, repeated.

### V29-17 — No test that does not drive the shipped product remains
**Why.** The decider's standing rule is that a test counts only if it drives the shipped binary as a person would, and a test that is not real is deleted. The tree still holds 100 unit tests in `vox-core` source and 29 test files that run nodes in-process rather than the shipped `vox`, and v0.2.8's release gate reported them as passing suites.
**Acceptance.** No unit test remains in any crate's source; every in-process test that backs a product claim is replaced by a proof that drives the shipped `vox` binary, and every other in-process test is deleted.
**Validation.** A count of `#[test]` in `crates/*/src` is zero; every remaining test file references the shipped binary; each replaced claim has a real-binary proof that fails when the behaviour it covers is broken.

### V29-18 — A rotated sender key does not freeze its sender while a member is offline
**Why.** When one author's messages in a room cross 1,000, its sender key rotates, and delivering the new key (`deliver_rekeys_for`, run from the post and on every tick) dialled each absent member inline on the node's actor, up to 10s an attempt. Measured on the real binaries with one member offline, 1,100 posts took 2,025s: posts 1–999 took about 12ms each, and every post from the 1,000th took 10–80s.
**Acceptance.** With a member offline, an author keeps posting at its normal rate across and after a sender-key rotation; automatic key delivery to an absent member never waits on the actor, while a command the person issued (consent, trust, revoke) still reports its delivery outcome.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof in which one member is offline and another posts past the rotation threshold shows no post taking over 1s, and the same proof on the tree before the fix shows the stall.

### V29-19 — A consent given just after a room opens reaches a member who is online
**Why.** Measured through the real `vox tui`: Alice opens a room whose other member is online and runs `:consent grant` on them. For the first 10s after the room opens the answer is `no reachable peer — both must be online (or run your node)` within 0.75s, which is false and immediate (at 0, 3, 6 and 10s it failed; at 15s and later it succeeded). The same happens on the trees before and after V29-18, so it is not that change. A person told their peer is offline when it is not has no way to know that waiting would work.
**Acceptance.** An explicit consent to a member who is online succeeds whenever it is given after the room opens; when the member's address is not yet known the node finds it (or waits for it) rather than refusing, and `no reachable peer` is reported only after a real attempt to reach the member failed.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof drives `vox tui` to consent to an online member immediately after opening the room and reads `done`; the same proof on the tree before the fix reads `no reachable peer`.

### V29-20 — A member whose trust changed does not livelock its rooms' pushes
**Why.** On the v0.2.9 integration tree, `node_m19_untrust_lock_gate` failed 2 of 2: a push owed per room rather than per peer kept a member's node re-owing the same pushes, so its rooms never settled after an untrust. That evidence is in-process; the defect's effect on a person is not yet shown through the shipped binary.
**Acceptance.** After `vox trust remove` against a member who shares several rooms, every room's pushes settle and messages keep crossing in both directions.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof untrusts a member sharing several rooms and shows messages crossing afterwards, and the same proof on the tree before the fix shows the livelock.

### V29-21 — A member's node does not freeze when a room's anchor disappears
**Why.** Measured through the real binaries: when the room's creator (acting as an anchor) vanished mid-publish, each put to it waited out 20s on a dead relayed connection on the node's actor. One of Bob's posts took 29.5s (`busy 10008ms — filing a sync that finished`), and freezes of 30–80s were seen.
**Acceptance.** When an anchor vanishes, a member's node keeps answering: no command waits more than 1s behind a publish to the vanished anchor.
**Validation.** A real-binary proof removes the anchor mid-publish and shows no post over 1s and no `busy` line over 1s, and the same proof on the tree before the fix shows the stall.
